Cricket in Australia

From national tradition to modern entertainment

Cricket has long been part of Australia’s sporting identity. This visualisation explores how the sport is connected to place, stadiums, scoring evolution, international success and the rise of the Big Bash League.

01 / Stadium Culture

Where cricket lives

Cricket in Australia is strongly connected to major cities and large public venues.

The map shows how major cricket stadiums are distributed across Australia.

Key idea

Cricket’s popularity is partly geographic, anchored by large venues and city pride.

02 / Venue Scale

The MCG dominates the cricket stage

Stadium capacity helps explain why some venues carry more symbolic weight than others.

The MCG sits in its own category compared with other Australian cricket venues.

Reading the chart

Longer lines represent larger capacities.

03 / Venue Tiers

Most venues are much smaller

Grouping stadiums into capacity tiers shows that mega-scale venues are rare.

This affects crowd potential and the atmosphere of major cricket events.

Why this matters

Venue size affects attendance records and commercial potential.

04 / The Game Became Faster

From patience to pace

Traditional Test cricket rewards patience and endurance.

ODI cricket changed the rhythm of the sport by encouraging faster scoring.

Story link

Faster scoring prepared audiences for shorter T20 cricket.

05 / T20 Scoring

BBL scoring has become faster

The Big Bash League shows how short-format cricket pushes scoring rates upward.

This chart uses BBL match data to show the change in runs per over across seasons.

Key insight

The newest BBL seasons have produced some of the highest scoring rates in the dataset.

T20 cricket is spread across major Australian cities

The Big Bash League connects cricket to major Australian cities through franchise teams.

Key insight

Modern cricket is both a sporting competition and a city-based entertainment network.

Cricket is no longer the same game

Australia’s cricket identity still depends on stadiums, tradition and national success. But the modern game is faster, more global and increasingly shaped by T20 entertainment.
